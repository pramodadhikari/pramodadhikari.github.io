FEATURED PROJECT / RM1.3

70 years of mountain climate.
At 1.33 km

RM1.3 is a convection-permitting regional climate dataset for the Rocky Mountain region. It resolves terrain and atmospheric structure at scales needed to study neighboring valleys, mountain slopes, and watersheds.

I led the modeling project from conception through execution: configuring the model, designing the simulations, automating workflows, running parallel computations, managing petabyte-scale output, and evaluating the resulting climate fields with observations.

Physics and climate forcing

The WRF simulations use ERA5–CONUS404 forcing for the historical climate. The future experiment uses a pseudo-global-warming (PGW) approach with CESM2 Large Ensemble changes under SSP3-7.0. The horizontal grid contains 782 × 1022 points.

Weather at multiple timescales

The archive includes selected wind, precipitation, temperature, and water-vapor fields every 5 minutes, 81 surface fields hourly, and 18 three-dimensional fields every 3 hours.

APPLICATIONS & NEXT STEPS

A foundation for impact research and AI

Snow and mountain water

The archive supports research on natural snow availability at ski areas and provides atmospheric forcing for watershed and streamflow modeling. My summit presentation highlights a 70-year WRF-Hydro application and our published ski-area study.

High-impact weather

Ongoing applications highlighted in the presentation include damaging winds, hail and flooding, and extreme precipitation. High-frequency fields allow researchers to examine event evolution within the longer climate record.

Physics-based data for AI/ML

RM1.3 offers a multi-decadal library of physically consistent atmospheric states for training and testing models over complex terrain. The presentation identifies fire and smoke prediction, including SmokeCast, as an application direction.

The next step is to curate targeted, analysis-ready training datasets and evaluate AI/ML performance across historical and warmer climates. These applications are at different stages of research and development.

My experience spans WRF and WRF-Chem, dynamical downscaling, model evaluation, and large-scale climate analysis. I use NCAR computing systems including Derecho and Casper, PBS job workflows, and Python tools such as xarray to process regional climate datasets and collaborate across disciplines.
