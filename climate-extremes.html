What does warming mean for mountain communities?

I use regional climate simulations to examine changes in heavy precipitation, snow availability, and mountain water resources. My work connects the physical changes with questions about flood risk, winter recreation, and climate adaptation.

GEOPHYSICAL RESEARCH LETTERS / 2025

Extreme precipitation at 2°C and 3°C of global warming

Our study uses 25 CMIP6 simulations, bias-corrected and dynamically downscaled to 9 km, to compare western U.S. precipitation extremes at global warming levels of 2°C and 3°C above pre-industrial conditions.

Projected frequency increases for historical 50-year Rx1day events. Rx1day is the annual maximum one-day precipitation amount.

Why it matters: A fixed warming level helps compare risks across models with different climate sensitivities. Events considered rare in the historical climate can become much more frequent, informing how communities assess flood exposure and infrastructure resilience.

This paper uses the 9 km downscaled CMIP6 ensemble, a separate modeling framework from RM1.3. Results describe model projections and their uncertainty.

NPJ CLIMATE AND ATMOSPHERIC SCIENCE / 2026

Ski-area resilience as winters shrink

Our regional Earth system modeling study examines natural snow availability and snowmaking conditions at Rocky Mountain ski areas. My summit presentation highlights natural ski-day analysis across 36 major ski areas as an application of RM1.3.

Projected losses vary substantially by location. Higher-elevation areas and places with more historical snowfall generally retain greater snow security. Colorado Rockies resorts tend to face smaller losses in season length and snowmaking potential than areas closer to the Pacific coast, despite stronger projected warming.

Why it matters: Local terrain and baseline snow conditions shape the consequences of warming. These differences help winter-recreation communities identify where natural snow, early-season snowmaking, and rain-on-snow changes may create the greatest pressures.
